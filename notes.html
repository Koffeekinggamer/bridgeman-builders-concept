Studio memorandum

Copy, design, and what to do next.

Prepared as a working sample against the current site at bridgemanbuild.wpenginepowered.com. The live pages are a fair start. They do not yet sound, or look, like a builder a serious client would trust with a ridgeline lot.

What is wrong with the current site

  1. The language is rented. “Luxury Homes, Designed Around You.” “Start Your Journey.” “Building Trust, One Home at a Time.” Any builder from Phoenix to Portland could run the same lines. High-end clients hear that as a template.
  2. The photography is stock. Unsplash credits, a file named demo.jpg, a generic lake aerial. Luxury clients decide in the first three seconds whether the work looks real. Stock says it isn’t.
  3. The social proof is hollow. The same testimonial appears twice. One is labeled “Testimonial Test.” Remove every invented review. An empty page is more expensive-looking than a fake one.
  4. Half the site is unfinished. Services, Gallery, and Contact have no real content. Several buttons loop back to the homepage. Empty rooms are fine in a house under construction. They are fatal on a website asking for a custom-home budget.
  5. There is no one on the page. Corey is the product. The current site never names him. Principal-led is the advantage over larger Greenville and Asheville firms. Use it.
  6. The word “luxury” is doing too much work. If the materials, the process, and the photographs are right, you barely need the word. If they are not, the word cannot save you.

How the voice should work

Write as if Corey is speaking to one couple standing on their lot. Short sentences. Named places. No coaching-industry verbs (journey, empower, exceed). Prefer nouns: stone, grade, canopy, schedule, principal.

Design principles baked into this sample

  • Photography is eighty percent of a builder’s site. Full-bleed, dusk, interiors with a view, process close-ups. Never a collage of six small JPEGs.
  • Type should feel like a monograph, not a theme. A serif for headlines (Cormorant Garamond), a quiet sans for interface (Outfit). Wide letter-spacing on labels. No all-caps paragraphs.
  • Palette from the work: ink, warm paper, fieldstone, bronze, forest. Not navy-and-gold “luxury,” not construction orange, not stark gallery white.
  • Space is a finish. Large type, long margins, one idea per section. If a section needs a stock icon, delete the section.
  • One primary action: Inquire. Phone in the header because this client still calls. Do not sprinkle “Get a Free Quote” across the page.
  • Honesty is high-end. This sample labels concept photography as concept photography. Do not put generated or stock houses in a “Our Projects” grid. The right client will notice. The wrong client is not the client.
  • Name the territory. Landrum, Tryon, Greenville, Flat Rock, Asheville. Specificity reads as competence.
  • Mobile is the site. Most first visits will be a phone in a car after a lot tour. The hero, the phone number, and Inquire must work with a thumb.

What this sample is not

It is not a claim that Bridgeman has already built these houses. It is a direction: architecture, voice, and structure. Swap in real photographs, Corey’s bio in his own words, license numbers, and a working inbox before this goes on a real domain.

Recommended next steps

  1. Photograph whatever exists. A foundation, a chimney going up, millwork in the shop, Corey on a site walk. Process is more persuasive than an empty “Gallery.”
  2. Write a 120-word bio in Corey’s voice. Where he came from, why Landrum, how many jobs he will take in a year. We can tune it; he should draft it.
  3. Put a real email on the wall. hello@bridgemanbuilders.com (or the domain you keep). The current site has a phone and almost no way to write.
  4. Finish the empty pages or delete them. A four-page site that is complete beats a six-page site with rooms under construction.
  5. Buy the domain you want to keep for ten years. Bridgemanbuilders.com / bridgeman.build — pick one, redirect the rest. WP Engine URLs should never be public-facing.
  6. Do not add testimonials until they are real, named, and permitted. A single sentence from a client, with a last name and a town, is worth more than a carousel.
  7. When the first house is dry, hire a photographer who shoots architecture, not real estate. Dusk exteriors. Quiet interiors. Details. That day is the marketing budget.

Competitors to study, not copy

Bluestone (Asheville / Greenville), JCM Custom Homes, Gabriel Builders, Sineath. Notice: large photographs, named projects, restrained type, the principal on the page. Bridgeman should not pretend to be a thirty-year firm. Boutique and principal-led is the honest, more expensive position.
